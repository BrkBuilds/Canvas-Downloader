Before you hit record

Account & safety

  • Generate a throwaway access token named “Video demo”. Revoke it in Canvas the moment you stop recording — it will be on screen in Chapter 1.
  • Decide now whether real course names stay in. If not, pick a semester whose names you are happy to publish.
  • Sign out of anything personal in the browser tab you use for the token walkthrough.

App state

  • Record the packaged build, not streamlit run. Users should see exactly what they install.
  • Start logged out so Chapter 1 is genuinely the first launch.
  • Have a second, already-synced course folder ready for Chapters 4–6 — you cannot demo a sync against a folder that has never been downloaded.
  • Keep Show help text ON. The help cards are part of the product story.

Staging the Sync Review

  • A folder that is already current returns zero actionable rows — everything sits in Up to Date. There is no take to get here; the screen has to be staged or the chapter cannot be shot.
  • On a copy of a synced folder, run
    python scripts/seed_today_test.py "<folder>" --new 8 --updates 6
  • It deletes files with no backup. Use --dry-run first, and never point it at your real coursework.
  • Hand-edit one file so it lands in Edited Locally — that is the category that proves the safety claim.

Capture

  • App window at 1920×1080, 100% display scaling, so text is readable after compression.
  • Every page transition is masked by a short overlay by design — let it play, do not cut mid‑fade.
  • Record the long download and sync runs in full; you will speed‑ramp them in the edit.
  • Grab spare footage of the finished folder in Explorer/Finder and of NotebookLM ingesting. Both are cutaways you will want.
CH 00

Why this exists

Camera 00:00 → 01:15

Hi, I'm Birk. I study Business Administration and Information Systems at Copenhagen Business School, and for three years I did the same thing every single semester.

Open Canvas. Click into a course. Click into a module. Download a file. Click back. Download the next one. For every course, every week.

And by exam season I'd have this enormous folder that I didn't actually trust. I could never be sure I had everything, because there was no way to check without going through Canvas again by hand.

Then AI study tools showed up. Suddenly I wanted all of it as PDFs and plain text so I could feed a whole course into NotebookLM — which meant a second manual job stacked on top of the first one.

DeliverySlow down on “didn't actually trust”. This is the emotional beat of the whole video — the pain isn't the clicking, it's the doubt.

So I built Canvas Downloader. It pulls all of your Canvas courses onto your computer in one go, keeps them up to date with a single click, and converts everything into formats an AI tool can actually read.

It's free, it's open source, and it runs entirely on your own machine. Let me show you the whole thing — starting from a completely fresh install.

CH 01

Connecting to Canvas

Screen 01:15 → 02:25

This is the app on first launch. It needs two things from you, and only two: your school's Canvas address, and an access token.

The address you almost never have to look up — there's a searchable directory of 4,757 verified schools built in. Start typing, and it fills the field for you.

  • Open the app cold on the login page.
  • Click the picker beside Your Canvas URL.
  • Type copenhagen slowly — let the list narrow live on camera.
  • Click Copenhagen Business School. Pause on the URL field auto-filling.

It opens on the schools in your own country, and accents are optional — typing k-o-b-e-n-h-a-v-n finds Københavns Universitet. And if your school isn't in the list, that's fine: the field is a normal text field, so you just paste your Canvas address in instead.

  • Reopen the picker, type kobenhavn without the ø, show the hit.
  • Close it. Click into the URL field and type a couple of characters by hand to prove it's editable.
  • Restore the CBS address.
Why this beatThe picker is a shortcut, never a gate. Saying so out loud stops a student whose school is missing from concluding the app doesn't support them.

The token is a key you generate yourself, inside Canvas. In your browser: Account, Settings, scroll down to Approved Integrations, New Access Token. Give it a name, leave the expiry blank, generate it, and copy it.

That's it — paste it in, and connect.

  • Cut to the browser. Canvas → Account → Settings.
  • Scroll to Approved Integrations → + New Access Token.
  • Purpose: Canvas Downloader. Expiry blank. Generate Token.
  • Copy, cut back to the app, paste, click connect.
  • Hold on the course list loading.

One thing worth saying about that token, because you should be suspicious of any app that asks for one. It's stored in your operating system's own password vault — Windows Credential Manager, or the macOS Keychain — never in a plain text file. And it's only ever sent to one place: your own university's Canvas server.

There's no backend here. No analytics, nothing phoning home. The app talks to your school and to nobody else.

  • Stay on the loaded course list while you say this — don't visualise it, it'll look defensive.
Portfolio signalCredential handling, stated plainly and without hedging. Reads as security literacy to an engineer and as trustworthiness to a student. Cheapest 15 seconds in the video.

Here's every course you're enrolled in. You can flip between your favourites and the full list, search it, and select courses individually — or just take all of them.

CH 03

Building an AI study pack

Screen 04:15 → 06:35

Now the other door. Custom Download is where you decide exactly what comes down and what shape it arrives in — and it's four cards.

Card one is the files themselves. Everything your teacher uploaded, or just slides and PDFs if you want the noise gone. And either mirroring Canvas's modules, or flat — every file loose in one folder.

  • Back to the course list. Select one course this time.
  • Click Custom Download.
  • Card 1 — Course Files & Organization. Click between All Files and Slides & PDFs.
  • Click between With Subfolders and All in One Folder.

Card two is the part I'm proudest of. Canvas holds a lot that isn't a file at all — your assignment briefs, the announcements, the quizzes, the syllabus, discussion threads, and your own grades and teacher feedback.

The app exports all of it as readable documents, with any attachments, so what lands on your disk is genuinely a one-to-one copy of the course rather than just its file tab.

  • Expand Card 2 — Canvas Content.
  • Toggle each of the six on: Assignments, Announcements, Quizzes, Syllabus, Discussions, Submissions (Results).
  • Show Match Course Folder structure vs In Separate Folders.
Portfolio signal“Everything that isn't a file” is the bit that shows you modelled the domain instead of just hitting the files endpoint. Say it as a design decision, not a feature list.

And card three is the one that changed how I study. Turn these on, and every time you download or sync, the app converts your materials into formats AI tools can read.

PowerPoints become PDFs. Excel sheets become a PDF and a structured data file with every cell and formula in it. Canvas pages become plain text. Videos become audio, which matters because most AI tools won't take a video, and the audio is ten to twenty times smaller.

  • Expand Card 3 — Optimize for AI Tools.
  • Enable: PowerPoint → PDF, Excel → PDF & AI Data, Canvas Pages → Plain Text, Video → Audio.
  • Hover one card to show the tooltip.
Say once, don't dwellOffice conversions drive the real desktop app, so PowerPoint / Word / Excel need to be installed. If they aren't, those files are simply left alone.

Run it — and this is the payoff. Open the folder, select everything, drag it into NotebookLM.

No converting files one at a time on some sketchy website. One course, fully readable, in one drag.

  • Start the download. Speed‑ramp through the run and the conversion phase.
  • Completion screen → Open Folder.
  • Select all → drag into a NotebookLM notebook. Hold on the ingest.
  • Optional: ask it one question about the course and show a real answer.
Worth the extra 10sIf the notebook answers a genuine question about the course, that shot alone justifies the chapter. Have the question ready so you're not typing on camera.

And if you'll want that same setup again — save it as a preset, and it's one click next time.

  • Open the presets hub, save the current configuration under a name, show it appear as a card.

CH 04

Staying current: Quick Sync

Screen 06:35 → 08:10

Downloading is the easy half. The hard half is Monday morning, when you don't know what your professors added over the weekend.

That's what Sync mode is for. You link a Canvas course to a folder on your computer — that's a course pair — and from then on the app remembers every single file it ever gave you for that folder.

  • Sidebar → Sync Course Folders.
  • Click Add Course, pick an already-synced folder.
  • Show the course being auto-detected from the folder.
  • Confirm. The pair appears on the Sync List.

You can save pairs on their own, or group several together — I keep one called Semester 1 — and load the whole group next time in one click.

  • Add a second pair so the list has two.
  • Click Save as Group, name it Semester 1.
  • Open Saved Groups & Pairs, show the saved cards, close it.

Then it's one button. Quick Sync goes out to Canvas, compares it against what you already have, and brings down only what's genuinely new.

N files. That's my week.

  • Click Quick Sync.
  • Let the run play, then land on the completion screen.
  • Expand a course card to show the file names that arrived.
Shoot first, then say the numberRecord the run, read the count off the completion screen, and dub the line to match. Any number written here in advance is a guess, and a guess that contradicts what's on screen costs you more trust than the whole chapter buys.

And the rules here matter more than the speed, so let me be exact about them.

Quick Sync will never overwrite a file you've annotated or edited. It will never bring back a file you deliberately deleted. And it never deletes anything — if your teacher pulls a file off Canvas, your copy stays exactly where it is.

It only ever adds new files, and replaces files you haven't touched. That's the whole contract.

  • Show the safety rules in the help card, or hold on the completion screen.
  • Deliver this slowly. It is the trust moment of the entire video.
Portfolio signalA non-destructive-by-default sync engine with a stated contract. To an engineer this is the most technically interesting sentence you say — it implies per-file state, change detection and conflict handling, without you having to claim any of it.
CH 05

Full control: Analyze, Review & Sync

Screen 08:10 → 10:00

Quick Sync is the safe, fast option, and it's what I use most days. But sometimes you want to know everything that's different — and that's the other button.

Analyze compares your folder against Canvas file by file, and then shows you the full picture before it touches anything.

  • Same Sync List. Click Analyze, Review & Sync.
  • Hold on the analysis dashboard — let the course counter tick.

Every difference is sorted into a category, and each one has its own default.

New files your teacher added — ticked, they'll come down. Clean updates, where Canvas has a newer version and you haven't touched yours — also ticked, replaced in place. Edited locally is the interesting one: Canvas is newer, but you've written on your copy. That's unticked, and if you do take it, the new version arrives beside yours with NewVersion on the end. Your annotations are never overwritten.

  • Walk the review screen top to bottom, one category at a time.
  • On Edited Locally, expand the row and point at the default unticked state.
Staging reminderThis screen needs the seeded folder from the prep list. An unstaged run shows two categories and the chapter falls flat.

Then deleted locally — files you removed on purpose. Your deletion is respected; nothing comes back unless you ask for it. And deleted on Canvas is information only: your teacher took it down, you still have it, and the app leaves it alone.

You can tick and untick anything individually. And anything you never want to see again goes into Ignored, which you can undo later.

  • Show Deleted Locally and Deleted on Canvas.
  • Hover a file row → click the eye icon to ignore it.
  • Scroll to Ignored Files and restore it, so the round trip is visible.

Confirm, and it tells you exactly what it's about to do — how many files, how big, where they're going, and whether you've got the room.

And when that finishes, the folder isn't “probably fine”. You've seen every difference and decided on each one. It's genuinely up to date.

  • Click sync → hold on the Confirm Sync dialog: file count, total size, destination, free space.
  • Confirm. Speed‑ramp the run, land on the completion screen.
  • Optional cutaway: the folder in Explorer with the new files highlighted by date.
CH 06

The Today page

Screen 10:00 → 11:10

Once all of that is set up, you mostly stop thinking about it — and that's what the Today page is for.

You pick the courses you actually want kept current, once. Then you turn on daily sync.

  • Sidebar → Today's files.
  • Click Add courses, tick your saved pairs and the Semester 1 group.
  • Close the dialog, show the course cards.

From then on, the first time you open the app each day, it quietly fetches everything new before you've even clicked anything. Same engine as Quick Sync, so the same safety rules apply.

And this is what I actually look at in the morning: everything that arrived today, grouped by course. Open a file, or open the folder, right from here. No digging through Canvas, no wondering what's new.

  • Flip Daily auto-sync on — hold on the toggle turning green.
  • Click Quick Sync now to populate the list live.
  • Scroll Today's files. Click Open Folder on one card.
Small detail worth sayingThe day rolls over at 4 AM, so a late-night study session still counts as today. It gets a smile and it signals care.
CH 07

Lecture recordings

Screen 11:10 → 12:00

One more, because it's the thing people ask for most. A lot of courses post their lectures as Panopto recordings, and those are slow to rewatch and impossible to hand to an AI.

The app finds them and saves them into the course folder like any other file — as video, as audio, as a written transcript, as subtitles, or just a shortcut back to the lecture.

  • Custom Download → scroll to Card 4, Panopto Recordings.
  • Show the outputs: MP4, MP3, transcript .txt, subtitles .srt, shortcut.
  • Show the two layouts: alongside your files, or a separate Panopto Recordings folder.

Video and audio need no setup at all. Transcripts do a one-time model download, and then the speech recognition runs entirely on your own computer — nothing is uploaded anywhere.

Before your first recording the app also asks you to read a short notice about what it's doing and how to use it. And if you'd rather it never touched recordings at all, there's a single switch in Settings.

  • Open the transcription setup dialog briefly, show the model list, close it.
  • Show the acceptable-use notice.
  • Cut a transcript .txt open beside the lecture, if you have one recorded.
Portfolio signalOn-device inference with an explicit consent gate. Being visibly careful about the one legally interesting feature reads much better than pretending it isn't there.
CH 08

Settings worth knowing

Screen 12:00 → 12:30

Quickly, three settings you'll actually use. A default save location, so you're not picking a folder every time. A file size limit — mine skips anything over 500 megabytes, which keeps the huge lecture videos out unless I ask for them. And notifications, so you know a sync finished without watching it.

Everything else you can leave alone.

  • Sidebar → Settings.
  • Point at default save location, the size limit toggle and its MB field, and notifications.
  • Close. Do not open the developer or health sections.

Deliberately left out — and what to say if asked

These are all real behaviours and all interesting, and every one of them would stall the video. Keep them for the written guide and for the comments.

  • Renamed-on-Canvas files — the app matches on content rather than filename, so a rename doesn't produce a duplicate.
  • Ignore, then delete locally, then re-download — and every other permutation of the category rules.
  • Folder scope — a folder downloaded as “Slides & PDFs” keeps that shape on every future sync.
  • Sync history — the last runs, per course, with what each one did.
  • The manifest — the per-folder database that makes all of the above possible.
  • Conversion edge cases — what happens to a file you edited that then gets reconverted.

If a comment asks about any of them, that's your next short-form video. Don't answer it in this one.

Two audiences, one script — how the split works

Nothing in the script addresses employers directly, and it shouldn't — a student watching would feel it immediately. The portfolio layer is carried entirely by four moments that a technical viewer reads differently from a student:

  • Ch 1 — credential storage and “no backend”. Security posture.
  • Ch 3 — exporting what isn't a file. Domain modelling.
  • Ch 4 — the non-destructive sync contract. The heaviest one; it implies an entire engine.
  • Ch 7 — local inference behind a consent gate. Judgement under legal ambiguity.

A student hears four reassurances. An engineer hears four design decisions. Neither has to be told which they're getting.

For the version you send to employers, the only change worth making is a pinned comment or description linking the repo and naming the stack. Leave the video itself alone.

Numbers used in the script — verify before recording
  • 4,757 verified schools — current as of v2.0.2. Re-check if the institution list is regenerated before launch.
  • Five Quick Download presets — Complete Canvas Download, Daily study pack (Optimized), 100% AI & NotebookLM Ready, Slides & PDFs Only, Files Only.
  • Six Canvas Content types — Assignments, Announcements, Quizzes, Syllabus, Discussions, Submissions (Results).
  • Eight AI conversions — unpack archives, PowerPoint→PDF, legacy Word→PDF, Excel→PDF+data, Canvas pages→text, code→txt, gather web links, video→audio.

Counts from your own runs are marked N and are deliberately blank. Two numbers stay yours to confirm rather than mine to supply: the 500 MB size limit in Chapter 8 should be whatever you actually have set, and drop the Apple Silicon / macOS 14+ line from the close if the Mac build isn't shipping at launch.
