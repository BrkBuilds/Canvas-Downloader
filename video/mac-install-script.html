Shooting script

Canvas Downloader on a Mac, first run

Everything to say, in order, keyed to the dialog that is on screen when you say it. Target length under three minutes. Every quoted line below is the wording macOS and the app actually use, lifted from the shipped setup guide.

Direction: face or no face

Face at the top and at the Gatekeeper block. Screen only for everything else.

The written guide already answers "which button". The one thing it cannot answer is "should I trust this?", and that is the only real drop-off point in the whole flow: macOS says Apple could not verify the app, and the viewer has to decide about a stranger's software. A person saying "I made this, it is free, here is why Apple blocks it" settles that. A text overlay cannot.

From "double-click the app" onward the screen is the information, and a face in the corner competes with it at exactly the wrong moment. Cut to full screen and stay there. If you want the face during the block explanation, use a small picture-in-picture and drop it the second you say "click Done".

Both videos already on the channel are screen only, so this keeps the series consistent and spends the new element where it earns something.

Before you press record

This Mac is clean once. Most of these dialogs appear on the first run and never again.

  1. Run sw_vers and note the version.The flow genuinely differs. macOS 15 Sequoia and later go through System Settings and get the App Data dialog. macOS 14 Sonoma and earlier use right-click then Open, and never see App Data at all. Say which one you are on, out loud.
  2. Check whether Microsoft Office is installed.No Office means dialogs 3, 4 and 5 never appear and cannot be filmed. Install it first, or accept that the video omits them and say so.
  3. Pick a course that really contains .pptx, .docx or .xlsx files.No conversion means no Office prompts and no App Data prompt. The permission batch is triggered by the conversion, not by the download.
  4. Record on the Mac itself with Cmd+Shift+5. Never through NoMachine.NoMachine shows you a compressed remote view, and recording that captures the compression. Record locally at full quality and transfer the file afterwards.
  5. Clear the frame of anything personal.Your Canvas token, your school name, course names, the account name in the menu bar. A fresh macOS user account is the cleanest way, and it re-arms every permission as a side effect.
  6. Start recording before the first double-click, every time.Even on a rehearsal. Screen recording costs nothing and some of these dialogs are awkward to bring back.

The script

Spoken lines in the serif. Overlay is the text you burn into the picture, using the literal button name macOS shows.

01Cold openon camera

Canvas Downloader is free and open source, which means I do not pay Apple's ninety-nine dollars a year to sign it. So macOS is going to block it once, on the very first launch, and then ask you a handful of permission questions.

None of it is complicated, but it is much easier to watch than to read. Two minutes, start to finish. Everything from here is my screen.

02Installscreen

Double-click the DMG file you downloaded. Drag Canvas Downloader onto the Applications folder, then eject the disk image. That is the install done.

OverlayDrag to ApplicationsEject
03The blockface, or PiP
On screen "Apple could not verify 'Canvas Downloader' is free of malware that may harm your Mac or compromise your privacy."

Here it is. And read what it actually says: macOS is not telling you it found something wrong with this app. It is telling you it has not checked, because the app is not signed with a paid Apple certificate. That is the whole reason this dialog exists.

Every line of Canvas Downloader is public on GitHub if you want to look at it.

Click Done. Not Move to Trash.

OverlayClick Donenot "Move to Trash"

Hold this beat. It is the only place in the video where somebody decides whether to keep going.

04Open Anywayscreen
On screen "Canvas Downloader was blocked to protect your Mac."

Now open the Apple menu, System Settings, then Privacy and Security. Scroll all the way down to Security. You will see a line saying Canvas Downloader was blocked. Click Open Anyway.

macOS asks for your Mac login password to confirm. That is its standard check for any app from an independent developer, not something this app is asking for. Type it in, click Open Anyway, and it opens.

If you are on macOS 14 Sonoma or older, you can skip the Settings detour completely: right-click the app, choose Open, then Open again. Apple removed that shortcut in macOS 15.

OverlaySystem Settings, Privacy & SecurityOpen AnywaymacOS 14 or older: right-click, Open
05Notificationsscreen
On screen "'Canvas Downloader' Would Like to Send You Notifications"

Click Allow. Canvas Downloader sends one banner, when a download or a sync finishes, so you can walk away and get told when it is done. It never sends anything else.

OverlayAllow
06Log in to Canvasscreen

The app asks for two things: your school's Canvas address, and a personal access token.

For the address, click Find your institution and start typing your school's name. It fills the address in for you. If your school is not on the list, just paste the address yourself. Every Canvas works, listed or not.

For the token, click Get a token. That opens your own Canvas settings page. Go to Approved Integrations, New Access Token, and copy it the moment it appears, because Canvas only shows it once. Paste it in, and you are logged in.

OverlayFind your institutionGet a tokenCanvas shows the token once

Blur or crop the token itself. Even a revoked one on camera is a bad look in a video about trust.

07First download, the permission batchscreen

Pick a course and start a download. Then stay at your Mac for about a minute. Canvas Downloader asks macOS for everything it needs up front, in one batch, so nothing interrupts the run later.

The dialogs arrive roughly in the order below. Let the footage decide. Cut your overlays to the order that actually happened rather than to this list.

07.1App DatamacOS 15+ only
On screen "Canvas Downloader" would like to access data from other apps. Keeping app data separate makes it easier to manage your privacy and security.

Allow. This one sounds bigger than it is. When the app turns a PowerPoint into a PDF, it works inside Microsoft Office's own scratch folder, and macOS counts that as another app's data. All of it happens on your Mac.

This is the only dialog that comes back. Apple designed this particular consent to expire when the app quits, so you will see it once per session, right at the start of your first download. One click, and the rest of the session is covered.

If you would rather never see it again, add Canvas Downloader to Full Disk Access in Privacy and Security. Worth doing if you use the daily sync.

OverlayAllowthe only one that returns, once per session
07.2Downloads folder
On screen "Canvas Downloader" would like to access files in your Downloads folder.

Allow. That is simply the folder you chose to save into. If you picked Documents or Desktop instead, you will see this for that folder.

OverlayAllow
07.3Microsoft PowerPoint
On screen "Canvas Downloader" wants access to control "Microsoft PowerPoint". Allowing control will provide access to documents and data in "Microsoft PowerPoint", and to perform actions within that app.

Allow. This is the PDF conversion. The app opens each PowerPoint, exports a PDF next to it, and closes it again. Nothing is uploaded anywhere.

OverlayAllowthis is the PDF conversion
07.4 - 07.5Word, then Excel
On screen "Canvas Downloader" wants access to control "Microsoft Word". Then the same for "Microsoft Excel".

The same dialog for Word, then Excel. Same answer, same reason.

And if you do not have Office installed, none of these three appear at all, and your files are simply kept exactly as they are.

OverlayAllowno Office? these never appear
08System Eventsafter the run finishes
On screen "Canvas Downloader" wants access to control "System Events".

One more, and this one turns up after the download has already finished, so do not be surprised by a dialog at the end.

Allow. This is the tidy-up. It closes the Word and PowerPoint windows the app opened for the conversions, and clears its temporary files out of your Office recent-files list. It never touches documents you opened yourself.

And if you miss it, nothing is lost. Office just stays open and you can quit it by hand.

OverlayAllowarrives AFTER the run

Do not cut this beat for time. It is the dialog people meet after they have walked away, and the setup page was rewritten specifically because it used to be explained in the wrong place.

09Closeon camera, optional

That is the whole setup. Every download and sync from here runs hands-free. The only thing you will ever see again is that App Data dialog, once per session, unless you granted Full Disk Access.

Do not say
  • "Do not worry, it is safe" or "it is not a virus". Naming the doubt is what plants it. Say what the permission is for, then move on.
  • "Just click Allow on everything." It sounds careless and it teaches a bad habit. One plain sentence per dialog about what it is for.
  • Any apology for the dialogs. You are not sorry. Apple charges ninety-nine dollars a year and you chose to keep the app free. Explain, do not apologise.
  • "LMS". Students say Canvas. So does every other surface of this product.
  • Anything you have not seen on screen in this take. Read the dialog actually in front of you and match its wording exactly. Order and text shift between macOS versions.
