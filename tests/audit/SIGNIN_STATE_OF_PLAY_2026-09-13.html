The two new ways in

A critical review of “Sign in with Canvas” and the browser-extension handoff, one commit after they shipped: what is genuinely right, what is broken, and what a student meets that you have not met because you only tested the first five minutes.

Baseline, measured tonight. Full suite 4,844 passed, 45 skipped in 6m21s. Architecture audit 0 violations across 72 Python and 8 CSS files. The browser_login mutation pass is still running as this is written; the commit notes it had not been re-run since the last round of fixes, and that gap is now being closed.

Everything below is either read out of the source with a file and line, or driven against the real thing. Where something is reasoned rather than measured it says so, and names the measurement that would settle it.

The short answer

The engineering underneath is the best work in this repository. The credential-as-a-value decision is correct and is the reason a change this large did not fracture the app: thirteen CanvasManager construction sites are untouched because the value carries the answer, and core/canvas_auth.py is the only place a bearer header is written. That is the fix for this codebase’s most expensive recurring defect, applied before the defect happened rather than after.

What is not finished is everything around the credential: the route you ship that nobody can install, the copy that still sends a new student to a page their school has closed, a privacy policy that now contradicts itself and the app, and a logout that leaves more behind than it did before this sprint. None of that is visible from a successful sign-in, which is exactly why five minutes of testing did not find it.

The single most important sentence in this document: your credential lifetime went from about 120 days to about 1 day, and several decisions elsewhere in the app were tuned for the 120. That is where long-term use will hurt, and it is finding 6.

What is genuinely right, stated once so it is not re-litigated

Keep

The credential is a value, not a code path

A distinct frozen type rather than a str subclass, so a misuse fails loudly at one site instead of silently degrading to an unauthenticated request that reports as “your token was revoked”. The hash=False on cookies is load-bearing and correctly reasoned.

Keep

Cookie scoping is structural, not remembered

Both client builders install cookies into a jar scoped to the Canvas host, so the redirect onto the content CDN drops them by construction. A bare cookies={…} dict would have forwarded a complete login to inst-fs. This is the difference between a rule and a habit.

Keep

is_login_redirect reads the chain, not the response

The best catch of the sprint. Outside the API an expired session is a 302 to /login that ends 200 with 45 KB of identity-provider HTML, and the download engine would have written that into lecture.pdf. Two holes in the old Content-Type guard (Canvas reporting no size, and files that legitimately are HTML) let it reach disk. One definition, matched on path alone, deliberately host-blind.

Keep

The synthetic-module runtime for the listener

A module the file watcher cannot unload is the right answer to an orphaned socket, and the write-up of why three independent “it worked” signals all agreed with a broken product is worth more than the fix.

Findings, in the order they should be worked

The number is the priority, not a catalogue index. The stripe is how a student meets it: red is met by everyone, amber by some, grey by few.

01

The extension route is advertised in the app and cannot be obtained

Ships broken

“Use the Canvas tab in my browser” renders unconditionally on the login page, and the card under it reads “Install the free Canvas Downloader Connector extension”. There is no store listing, no link anywhere in the app or on the site, and the extension/ folder is referenced by neither build spec nor the Inno script — so it is not in the installer either. A user who installs the .exe has no path to that folder at all.

What they get instead: the button opens their Canvas in a tab, arms a three-minute listener, and after 180 seconds shows “Nothing came back from Chrome”. The failure card is well written and blames the right things, which makes it worse — it reads as the student’s fault.

Three ways out, in descending order of work: publish to the Chrome Web Store; ship extension/ inside the installer with an in-app page that explains the unpacked load; or hide the button until one of those exists. Note that the manifest asks for cookies plus optional_host_permissions: ["*://*/*"], which is the combination Web Store review scrutinises hardest — budget for a justification, not a rubber stamp.

Where: ui/auth.py:4174 (the button), ui/auth.py:2210 (the copy), extension/README.md (“the store listings are not set up yet”), Canvas_Downloader.spec & Canvas_Downloader_Setup.iss (no reference)

02

The first thing a new student reads still tells them to get a token

Ships broken

On a fresh install the login page’s only orienting line, above the fold, is: “New here? Find your school, get a token, log in - about 2 minutes.” followed by “Your token is stored only on this device…”. It renders when _first_run = not os.path.exists(CONFIG_FILE).

For the population this entire sprint exists for, step two of that three-step instruction is impossible. The strip predates the new routes and was not revisited when they landed. Everything below it is correct — the button is above the token field, the divider names the relationship — but the headline still describes the old app.

Where: ui/auth.py:4079-4090

03

The published privacy policy contradicts itself, and the app

Live on the site

docs/privacy.html line 601 still says: “We do not modify, submit, or upload anything to your Canvas account. The app only reads and downloads.” Ninety lines above it, added in this same commit, the page describes the app creating an access token in the user’s Canvas account. That is a POST to /api/v1/users/self/tokens, and extend() is a PUT. Both are writes to the student’s account.

The commit message repeats the claim too (“Still read-only”). It is no longer true, and the honest framing is easy and still flattering: the app reads your course data and writes exactly one thing — a key in your own name that you can see and delete. Say that.

The same file mentions the browser extension and the loopback listener zero times. That is the disclosure gap that matters for the Store, which links this page.

Where: docs/privacy.html:601 vs docs/privacy.html:515-523

04

Logout does not revoke the token it minted, or clear its bookkeeping

Shared machines

core/token_mint.py exposes mint, extend, days_left, renewal_threshold_days and due_for_renewal. There is no revoke. So signing out of the app leaves a live access token, valid for up to 120 days, sitting in the student’s Canvas account under the name Canvas Downloader. The app holds both its id and its value; DELETE /api/v1/users/self/tokens/:id is one documented call.

This contradicts the feature’s own stated justification — that a token is better than a cookie because it is “revocable” — and the rule file’s own section titled Logging out means logging out.

Second half, and it is the repo’s first standing rule broken between two sites written in the same sprint: the paste-a-token path clears minted_token_id, minted_token_expires_at, minted_token_days, minted_token_capped and token_source, with a comment explaining precisely why leaving them is wrong. Logout clears none of them. After a logout _token_was_minted() still answers true, so the next expiry arms a hidden browser renewal for a credential the user deliberately signed out of.

Where: ui/auth.py:6173-6230 (logout) vs ui/auth.py:4400-4407 (the paste path that gets it right)

05

Uninstalling leaves a complete login, and possibly a university password, on disk

Shared machines

[UninstallDelete] in the Inno script removes only {app}. The web view profile lives in %APPDATA%\CanvasDownloader\webview\, and since this sprint that folder holds the live Canvas and identity-provider session cookies and, because enable_password_manager now switches IsPasswordAutosaveEnabled on, any institution password WebView2 was allowed to save.

Before this sprint that directory held settings and history and the uninstaller was right to leave it. Its contents changed class and nothing revisited the uninstaller. MSIX is unaffected — the package container goes with the app — so this is the .exe track only, which is the smaller share of installs but the one a student is most likely to run on a shared laptop.

Where: Canvas_Downloader_Setup.iss:117-119; core/browser_login.py:179 (enable_password_manager)

06

The optimistic restore was tuned for a 120-day token and now runs a 1-day session

Long-term use

This is the answer to your actual question, and it is the one you cannot see by testing right after a sign-in.

_adopt_restored_credential validates the stored credential at launch. On a genuine auth rejection it routes to the login page; on anything else — offline, captive portal, a slow launch, a TLS intercept — it restores optimistically, setting is_authenticated = True. That is correct, well argued, and was correct when the stored credential was a token that lasts months: the premise is “it validated on a prior launch, so it is probably still good”.

For a browser session that premise inverts. A Canvas session is a rolling day, and the rolled cookie is only written back to the store inside _adopt_restored_credential itself — i.e. once per app launch. So for a student who opens the app weekly, the stored session is usually dead by the next launch, and the optimistic branch is usually wrong.

The consequence compounds. _restore_browser_session arms the silent renewal with if not st.session_state.get('is_authenticated') — so the optimistic branch suppresses the very renewal that would have fixed it. The app then runs on a dead session until the first Canvas call fails, which is a noisy recovery where a silent one was available. And the daily auto-sync hook sits downstream of that same flag (app.py:1043 stops unauthenticated sessions, :1060 fires the sync), which is the open report from 2026-09-07 about Today’s files running on an unconfirmed session — previously rare, now the ordinary weekly case.

Read from source, not measured. To settle it: store a browser credential, point the app at a host that times out rather than refuses (a listening socket that never answers), launch, and read whether browser_restore_pending was ever armed. The narrow fix is that browser mode should treat a non-auth failure as “arm the renewal and keep checking” rather than “declare signed in”, because unlike a token it has a live, local input to the renewal: the profile’s identity-provider session.

A second, cheaper improvement in the same area: the rolled cookie is written back only at launch, so the rule file’s claim that the session now lasts “a day after you last used it” is really “a day after you last launched it”. A download run holds a live jar for hours and throws it away. Writing it back when a run finishes closes that for free.

Where: ui/auth.py:1371-1456 (_adopt_restored_credential), ui/auth.py:1329-1358 (_restore_browser_session), core/canvas_logic.py:1312 (refreshed_credential), app.py:1053-1063

07

The listener outlives its window, and a caller that sends no Origin is accepted

Measured tonight

Driven against the real core.handoff, with controls that had to fail and did:

control, before start(): bound = False          <- port free, as it must be
start() -> 53127 | bound = True | waiting = True
after deadline: waiting = False | socket bound = True
ping after timeout -> {'app': 'canvas-downloader', 'waiting': False}
POST with NO Origin -> 200 b'{"ok": true}'
payload the app would adopt: {'host': 'evil.example.com', 'cookies': {...}}
control, page Origin -> 403 (refused, as designed)   <- the control works
after stop(): bound = False

Two facts. First, when the three-minute window expires the acceptance flag flips but stop() is never called, so the socket stays bound and answering for the life of the process. The module docstring and extension/README.md both say it “times out after three minutes”; the window does, the socket does not. It does close correctly on a successful handoff.

Second, _is_extension_origin accepts an absent Origin header. Item 3 of the module’s own threat model says “Extension origins only”; the code is “extension origins, or anything that sends no Origin at all”. A web page cannot exploit this — browsers always set Origin on a POST, and the page-origin control above confirms pages are refused — but any other program running as the user can, and adopt_pending_handoff then takes api_url from that payload and signs the app into whatever host it names.

Severity is bounded: it needs local code execution, and a local process running as the user could already read the credential store. But the direction is unusual — not “steal the credential” but “point the downloader at an account somebody else controls” — and the fix is small: keep the permissive rule on GET /ping, require a real extension origin on POST /canvas-session. Measure before tightening — confirm with the real extension in real Chrome that its POST carries Origin, because if it does not, this change kills the feature.

Where: core/handoff.py:147 (_is_extension_origin), :302 (do_POST), :405 (waiting), ui/auth.py:1806 (the host is adopted verbatim)

08

The card written to end the “extension says done, app says waiting” contradiction never renders

Dead code

_browser_notice_html('handoff_arrived') — “Got it. Chrome sent your Canvas sign-in over. Signing you in now.” — is defined at ui/auth.py:2321 and asserted by three lines of tests/test_handoff.py. No production caller ever passes that string. The tests call the formatter directly, so they pass against a state the app cannot reach. Exactly the trap the memory register calls “assert the expression, not the token”.

It matters because of a mechanism this repo already has written down: st.rerun() takes effect only on FINISHED_SUCCESSFULLY, so the previous screen stays painted through the next blocking run. The arrival tick calls st.rerun immediately, and the run that follows does validate_token() plus a mint attempt on the script thread. For those seconds the student is still looking at “One more click, over in Chrome — Waiting for your browser” while the extension shows green. That is the reported bug, in a narrower window.

The fix is the repo’s own pattern: paint handoff_arrived on the tick that sees the payload, fire the rerun on the next one. Arm the action, fire it a run later.

Where: ui/auth.py:2321 (defined), ui/auth.py:2392-2399 (the branch that should reach it), tests/test_handoff.py:1569,1593,1714

09

Two settings that govern consent have no user interface

Consent

keep_signed_in (default on) makes the app re-date session cookies inside WebView2 so a sign-in survives closing the app for 30 days. token_upgrade_enabled (default on) makes the app write an access token into the student’s Canvas account. Both are read from the settings file; nothing anywhere writes either one. There is no toggle in Settings, so neither can be declined.

Related: token_upgrade_blocked is a single global key, not per-institution, and is never cleared. A student who first signs in at a school that blocks tokens never gets the upgrade at a later school that allows it, and never gets it at their own school if the policy is reversed.

The disclosure that does exist is a single st.toast, once, four seconds, fired on the busiest run of the session. Nothing in Settings or the sidebar ever says which credential you are on, when it expires, or that a key named Canvas Downloader now exists in your account.

Where: ui/auth.py:1975 / :2003 (readers with no writers), ui/auth.py:2162-2169 (the toast)

10

The extension takes a cookie it does not need, for a reason that is not true

Privacy posture

_csrf_token is in both ACCEPTED_COOKIES and the extension’s WANTED, and extension/README.md justifies it as “so the app can create a long-lived key without another round trip to Canvas”. But mint() always performs that round trip — one GET /profile/settings — and reads the CSRF token from that jar, deliberately, because a restored credential never carries one. The handed-over copy is never used.

It is dropped at rest (to_storable keeps only session cookies) so nothing leaks. But an extension whose whole pitch is “here is precisely what it reads” should not read a third thing for a stated reason that is false, and Web Store review asks about exactly this. Drop it from both lists, or correct the README.

Where: core/handoff.py:90, extension/background.js:43, core/token_mint.py:279-295

11

A first sign-in waits up to 15 seconds behind a hidden window that cannot succeed

Friction

Already measured in the rule file against real CBS SSO: notice at 1.3s, the hidden window already sitting on the Entra login page at 3.3s, the window finally shown at 17.0s. The settle rule (four polls of an unchanged URL) is supposed to show it sooner and did not, because a real identity provider keeps rewriting the URL.

The silent phase exists to let a live SSO session renew Canvas’ day without a window. On a first sign-in there is nothing to renew — the profile is empty — so it is pure dead time, paid by exactly the people who have never used the app. SILENT_TIMEOUT is a constant, so a faster laptop makes the dead time longer, not shorter.

The discriminator already exists: the renewal path arrives through browser_restore_pending, the button through a click. Give the button a short clock (or none) and keep the long silent phase for the renewal.

Where: core/browser_login.py:84 (SILENT_TIMEOUT), :1017-1024, ui/auth.py:1503

The cheapest test you can run, and it answers your actual question

  1. Sign in with Canvas. Then open the settings file and confirm auth_method is browser, and read what happened to the mint: token_source: minted means CBS allowed it, token_upgrade_blocked: true means it refused, and neither key present means the upgrade never completed and is being retried on every sign-in forever.
  2. Close the app completely. Reopen it. It should sign in silently. Time it.
  3. Leave it for three days. Reopen. This is the launch that exercises the hidden renewal, and the one nobody has driven.
  4. Sign out, and then look in Canvas under Account → Settings → Approved Integrations. If Canvas Downloader is still listed, that is finding 4 in front of you.
